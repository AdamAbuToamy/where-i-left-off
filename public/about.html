Leave a checkpoint your future self can use.

Before you stop, write down what you know and what you will do next. “Continue the project” leaves you planning again. A specific action gives you somewhere to start.

  1. Record the state

    Note what is complete, what you last tried and what is blocked. For example: “The import reads the file, but rejects rows with an empty date.”

  2. Choose one next action

    Write an action you can begin without another planning session. Keep the smaller checks together in a checklist.

  3. Keep the context

    Add your notes and resource links. When you return, read the checkpoint and update it as the work progresses.

The details stay with the project.

Keep the current state, next step, priority, notes, checklist and useful links with each project. Use search and filtering to find the work you want to resume.

Open the app on your phone or computer and sign in to the same account. Allow your latest changes to synchronize before expecting them on another device.

Keep your checkpoint accurate as the work changes. A short, current note and one useful next action are often enough to make restarting easier.

A few useful answers.

How is a checkpoint different from a to-do item?

A to-do item records an action. A checkpoint also records the state behind it: what is finished, what failed and which resources you need. You can keep a checklist alongside that context.

Do I need an account?

This guide is public. Your workspace requires sign-in, and the examples here do not load anyone’s saved projects.

Can I return from another device?

Yes. Sign in to the same account on your phone or computer, and let changes synchronize before switching devices.

Which languages are supported?

The interface and this guide support English, Arabic and Hebrew. Use the language links at the top of this page to read the complete guide in your preferred language.
